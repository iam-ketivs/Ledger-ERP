LEDGER v4.6  -  Balance Sheet
31 August 2026


WHAT IT IS
==========
The one thing that spreadsheet template had which Ledger did not: a
formal balance sheet, in the shape a bank expects.

Money -> Balance Sheet. Two columns, generously spaced, printable to A4.

    ASSETS                          LIABILITIES & EQUITY
    Current assets                  Current liabilities
      Cash in hand                    Owed to suppliers
      Money owed by customers         Advances on undelivered orders
      Advances to staff               Customer credit balances
    Stock                           Owner's equity
      Raw material (rolls)            Owner's capital
      Finished goods                  Retained earnings
    -------------------------       -------------------------
    TOTAL ASSETS                    TOTAL LIABILITIES & EQUITY

Pick any date with "As at" - useful when a bank asks for the position at
the end of a particular month.


IT BALANCES, AND THAT IS THE POINT
==================================
A balance sheet that does not balance is worthless. Rather than plugging
a difference, owner's equity is worked out as assets minus liabilities
and then split between what you put in and what the trading has earned.
The two sides agree by construction.

Tested through every kind of entry - a credit sale, a credit purchase, a
supplier payment, an expense, an order advance. Both sides matched to the
paisa every time.


THREE NOTES UNDERNEATH
======================
  WORKING CAPITAL   what would be left if every short-term debt were
                    settled today from cash, receivables and stock
  CURRENT RATIO     short-term assets against short-term debts. Banks
                    look for 1.5 or better; below 1.0 means the debts
                    exceed what could cover them
  RETAINED EARNINGS every rupee the trading has earned since Ledger
                    began, less every expense

Your current ratio is around 0.59, which is low - your suppliers are
owed considerably more than your cash and stock could cover today. Worth
knowing before a bank tells you.


HONEST ABOUT WHAT IT IS NOT
===========================
A note on the sheet itself says so plainly: drawn from Ledger's records
and not independently audited, stock at weighted average cost, no
provision for depreciation on equipment or for doubtful debts.

Say that to a bank up front and it reads as competence. Let them
discover it and it reads as something else.


WHY NOT USE THE SPREADSHEET
===========================
That template is a manual form built for a software company - MRR,
hosting fees, implementation revenue. Every quarter you would retype
figures out of Ledger into it, and it would be stale the next day.

This reads your own records each time you open it. Nothing to fill in,
nothing to keep up to date.


WHO CAN SEE IT
==============
You only. It shows what you owe, what you are worth and what you have
drawn out.

INSTALL
=======
1. Rename your current "erp" folder to "erp-old". Keep it.
2. Put LedgerApp in its place, renamed "erp".
3. Create a folder inside called "instance".
4. Copy YOUR CURRENT erp.db into it.
5. Copy instance\uploads across as well.
6. Run build_exe.bat.
7. Settings should say v4.6.

No database change.


ONE THING TO REMEMBER
=====================
Your finished goods and several suppliers are still not entered, and
wages are dated to when you pay rather than when they were earned. The
balance sheet inherits both. Fix those and this becomes a document you
could hand to a bank without hesitating.
